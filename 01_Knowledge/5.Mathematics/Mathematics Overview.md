# Mathematics — Overview

## Scope

Pure mathematical machinery — kept deliberately separate from [[6.Statistics & Experimental Design]], which is about *applying* probabilistic reasoning to data and experiments. This subject is the toolkit; Statistics is one major consumer of it (so is ML&DL).

## Planned sub-areas (fill in as studied)

1. Linear Algebra — vectors, matrices, eigenvalues/eigenvectors, SVD (heavily used by ML&DL — PCA already links here conceptually)
2. Calculus — derivatives, gradients, chain rule (the machinery behind [[Gradient Descent]])
3. Probability Theory — distributions, expectation, variance, Bayes' theorem (the foundation Statistics builds on)
4. Discrete Mathematics — combinatorics, graph theory, logic (feeds DSA)
5. Optimization Theory — convexity, constrained optimization (the theory behind [[Gradient Descent]], [[Regularization]])

## Connections

- [[6.Statistics & Experimental Design]] — applies probability theory to real data and experiments
- ML & DL notes ([[Gradient Descent]], [[PCA]], [[Regularization]]) already assume this machinery informally; this subject is where it gets formalized properly
